# Customer Segmentation with K-Means

Customer segmentation using unsupervised machine learning to identify distinct purchasing behaviours and translate them into actionable business strategies.

## Business Objective

Retail customers do not contribute equally to revenue and do not exhibit the same purchasing behaviour.

The objective of this project is to transform transactional retail data into meaningful customer-level profiles and use K-Means clustering to identify groups with distinct patterns of recency, purchase frequency and monetary value.

The resulting segmentation can support differentiated strategies for customer retention, development and reactivation.

## Dataset

The project uses the **Online Retail** dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository.

The original dataset contains:

- **541,909 transaction records**
- **8 variables**
- Transactions between **1 December 2010 and 9 December 2011**

After data quality checks and cleaning, the analysis uses:

- **392,692 valid purchase transactions**
- **4,338 identifiable customers**

The raw dataset is not stored in this repository.

More information about the data source and access is available in the [dataset documentation](./data/README.md).

## Data Preparation

The initial data quality assessment identified:

- 135,080 records without a `CustomerID`
- 1,454 missing product descriptions
- 5,268 exact duplicate rows
- 10,624 transactions with non-positive quantities
- 2,517 transactions with non-positive unit prices

Customer-level segmentation requires transactions to be associated with identifiable customers and to represent valid purchase behaviour.

The final cleaning strategy therefore:

- Removes records without a `CustomerID`
- Removes exact duplicate records
- Excludes cancelled or returned transactions
- Retains only positive quantities
- Retains only positive unit prices
- Preserves the original dataset unchanged and applies transformations to a separate working copy

## Feature Engineering

Transactional data is transformed into one customer-level record using three behavioural dimensions:

### Recency

Number of days since the customer's most recent purchase.

Lower values indicate more recent engagement.

### Frequency

Number of unique purchase transactions made by the customer.

Higher values indicate stronger repeat purchasing behaviour.

### Monetary

Total monetary value generated by the customer.

Higher values indicate greater customer value.

Together, these features form an **RFM customer profile**.

## Distribution Treatment

The original RFM variables showed substantial skewness, particularly for Frequency and Monetary value.

| Feature | Original Skewness | After Log Transformation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recency | 1.25 | -0.38 |
| Frequency | 12.07 | 1.21 |
| Monetary | 19.34 | 0.40 |

A logarithmic transformation was applied to reduce the influence of extreme values while retaining high-value customers in the analysis.

The transformed features were subsequently standardised using `StandardScaler` so that all three dimensions contributed comparably to the distance calculations used by K-Means.

## Model Selection

Several clustering solutions from **k = 2 to k = 10** were evaluated using:

- **K-Means inertia / Elbow Method**
- **Silhouette Score**
- **Business interpretability**

The two-cluster solution produced the highest Silhouette Score:

- `k = 2` → **0.433**
- `k = 4` → **0.338**

However, the two-cluster solution created only a broad distinction between lower-value and higher-value customers.

The final model uses **four clusters** because it provides substantially more actionable customer profiles while maintaining reasonable mathematical separation.

Final Silhouette Score:

**0.338**

## Customer Segments

The final model identifies four distinct customer segments.

| Segment | Customers | Customer Share | Monetary Share | Median Recency | Median Frequency | Median Monetary |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **High-Value Loyal Customers** | 713 | 16.44% | 64.89% | 8 days | 10 | 3,722.82 |
| **Established Regular Customers** | 1,166 | 26.88% | 23.64% | 57 days | 4 | 1,345.20 |
| **Recent Occasional Customers** | 837 | 19.29% | 5.25% | 17 days | 2 | 480.31 |
| **Inactive Low-Value Customers** | 1,622 | 37.39% | 6.22% | 174.5 days | 1 | 296.09 |

## Key Findings

### High-Value Loyal Customers

Only **16.44% of customers generate 64.89% of total monetary value**.

They combine very recent purchasing activity with high frequency and high spending, making them the most strategically important group for retention.

**Potential actions:**

- Prioritise loyalty and retention initiatives
- Offer early access to new products or premium services
- Use personalised cross-selling and upselling
- Avoid unnecessary discounting that may reduce customer value

### Established Regular Customers

These customers represent **26.88% of the customer base** and contribute **23.64% of monetary value**.

They form a substantial base of repeat customers with potential to move towards the highest-value segment.

**Potential actions:**

- Strengthen loyalty
- Increase purchase frequency
- Develop relevant cross-selling opportunities
- Identify customers showing potential to migrate into the high-value segment

### Recent Occasional Customers

These customers have purchased recently but still show limited frequency and monetary value.

Their recent engagement creates an opportunity to develop the relationship before activity declines.

**Potential actions:**

- Encourage a second or subsequent purchase
- Use personalised product recommendations
- Develop onboarding or nurture campaigns
- Test communications designed to increase purchase frequency

### Inactive Low-Value Customers

This is the largest group, representing **37.39% of customers**, but it contributes only **6.22% of monetary value**.

**Potential actions:**

- Use low-cost reactivation campaigns
- Test targeted incentives
- Monitor response before allocating additional resources
- Avoid expensive retention initiatives without evidence of likely return

## Business Interpretation

The analysis shows that customer value is highly concentrated within a relatively small proportion of the customer base.

The four-cluster solution provides a more useful view than a simple high-value versus low-value distinction because it separates customers according to different stages of engagement and economic value.

This enables differentiated strategies for:

- Retention
- Customer development
- Reactivation
- Cross-selling
- Resource prioritisation

## Limitations and Next Steps

This segmentation focuses on Recency, Frequency and Monetary value.

Future development could incorporate additional behavioural dimensions such as:

- Average order value
- Product diversity
- Purchasing cadence
- Geographic behaviour
- Return behaviour
- Category preferences

Alternative clustering approaches could also be compared with K-Means to evaluate the stability and robustness of the identified segments.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how raw transactional data can be transformed into customer-level behavioural profiles and analysed with unsupervised machine learning.

Rather than treating clustering as an isolated modelling exercise, the analysis connects model results with customer behaviour, economic contribution and practical business decision-making.
